about me

As an undergraduate, I developed a strong interest in observational astronomy. At Princeton, I worked with Dr Melinda Soares-Furtado, then a PhD candidate, compiling a catalog of periodic variables in open clusters from Kepler K2 observations. Following that, I worked with Professor Saurabh Jha at Rutgers University on a senior honors thesis fitting the early time lightcurves of Type Ia supernovae from TESS data to search for potential companion signatures. After graduating with a Bachelor’s degree in Astrophysics at Rutgers University in 2021, I joined the Astrophysics PhD program at UMN.

Since starting at UMN, I’ve worked with Professor Michael Coughlin to develop a pipeline to automate ZTF follow-up observations of potential companions to gravitational wave sources detected by LIGO.

personal life

I spent the bulk of my childhood in a New Jersey suburb near Philadelphia and aspired to study astronomy from an early age; after graduating high school, I began my undergraduate degree at Rutgers University-New Brunswick, where I was a member of the Honors College. Moving to Minnesota has been the first time I’ve lived outside of New Jersey, and I’ve enjoyed discovering more about the Twin Cities in the time I’ve been here. In my spare time, I enjoy cooking, collecting records, watching films, playing the guitar, and reading.

news

Jun 05, 2026 I was awarded the Robert O. Pepin Fellowship by the University of Minnesota to support my graduate research over the summer.
Apr 28, 2026 I spent April 2026 in Italy as a visiting scholar at the Italian National Institute for Astrophysics (INAF), supported by the ACME Transnational Access program. While there, I worked on machine learning classifiers—including Random Forest and XGBoost models—and alert streaming tools using the Babamul Kafka consumer for rapid transient follow-up.
Oct 18, 2025 I gave a contributed talk at the AstroRob VIII workshop on autonomous robotic observatories. I spoke about our work modernizing NASA’s General Coordinates Network (GCN) to support rapid alert distribution for time-domain and multi-messenger astronomy.
Jul 22, 2025 My paper, IIb or not IIb: A Catalog of ZTF Kilonova Imposters, was accepted for publication by PASP. In this work, we present a catalog of Type IIb supernovae and other fast-evolving transients that can mimic kilonova light curves in wide-field surveys.
Jan 14, 2025 I attended the 245th AAS meeting in National Harbor, Maryland, where I presented a poster on identifying Type IIb supernovae and other kilonova imposters in ZTF data.

    An online framework for fitting fast transient light curves
    Tyler Barna, Brandon Reed, Igor Andreoni, and 7 more authors
    Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society, Jun 2024
    Abs DOI arXiv Bib HTML Code

    Rapid classification and characterization of multi-messenger sources discovered by synoptic sky surveys such as the Zwicky Transient Facility (ZTF) and the Vera C. Rubin Observatory’s Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST) requires prompt processing of alert streams. We present a scalable online framework integrated with the Nuclear Multimessenger Astronomy (NMMA) software package to perform parameter estimation on fast transients in near-real-time.

  4. PASP
    IIb or not IIb: A Catalog of ZTF Kilonova Imposters
    Tyler Barna, Christoffer Fremling, Tomás Ahumada, and 1 more author
    Publications of the Astronomical Society of the Pacific, 2025
    Abs arXiv Bib HTML

    We present a comprehensive catalog and systematic investigation of core-collapse supernova kilonova imposters identified by the Zwicky Transient Facility. Utilizing machine-learning photometric classifiers and spectroscopic follow-up, we quantify contamination rates and establish observational criteria to prevent misidentification during rapid gravitational-wave electromagnetic counterpart follow-up.
