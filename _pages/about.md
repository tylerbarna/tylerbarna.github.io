I study fast optical transients, especially kilonovae, the short-lived glow of neutron-star mergers, and build the software that finds them in survey data quickly enough to follow them up. I'm advised by [Michael Coughlin](https://cse.umn.edu/physics/michael-coughlin) and work within the [NMMA](https://github.com/nuclear-multimessenger-astronomy/nmma) collaboration and the Zwicky Transient Facility.

Two first-author papers cover most of that so far. The first describes an [online framework](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stae1164) that fits new ZTF transients to kilonova and GRB-afterglow models every night and flags the promising ones for follow-up. The second is a [catalog of kilonova imposters](https://doi.org/10.1088/1538-3873/adf578): the Type IIb supernovae and other transients that look like kilonovae early on, and photometric tests for ruling them out without a spectrum.

Since 2023 I've also been a graduate research assistant with NASA Goddard on the [General Coordinates Network](https://gcn.nasa.gov), where I work on the search tools for the GCN Circulars archive.

### background

As an undergraduate I worked with Melinda Soares-Furtado at Princeton on a catalog of periodic variables in open clusters from Kepler K2, and with Saurabh Jha at Rutgers on a senior honors thesis fitting the early light curves of Type Ia supernovae from TESS to look for signatures of a companion star. I graduated from Rutgers with a B.S. in astrophysics in 2021 and started the PhD program at Minnesota that fall.

I spent most of my childhood in a New Jersey suburb near Philadelphia; Minnesota is the first place I've lived outside New Jersey, and I've enjoyed getting to know the Twin Cities. In my spare time I cook, collect records, watch films, read, and play the guitar ([I built one](/projects/fun/guitar/)).
